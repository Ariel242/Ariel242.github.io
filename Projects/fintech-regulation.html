Overview

FinTech innovation consistently outpaces the laws written for a slower, bank-centered financial system. This research examines how leading jurisdictions have responded — how they balance financial stability, competition and consumer protection when regulating non-bank financial technology players.

The comparison spans four regulatory arenas — the EU, USA, UK and Israel — and integrates three regulatory domains that are usually studied separately:

Three Regulatory Domains

Open Banking

From the EU's PSD2 and the UK's CMA-mandated model to Israel's Financial Information Services Law — how forced data-sharing broke the banks' monopoly on customer information and opened the market to third-party providers.

AML Standards

The FATF recommendations as a global baseline, and their national implementations — from FinCEN's money-transmitter regime in the US to the Bank of Israel's Proper Conduct of Banking Business Directive 411 on money-laundering risk.

Banking Regulation

Prudential supervision in the age of non-bank competitors: licensing models, capital requirements, safeguarding of client funds, and how supervisors integrate FinTech players into national payment infrastructures.
