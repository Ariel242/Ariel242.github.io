Algorithmic Fairness in Credit Scoring

Do the words borrowers write matter — and do they make credit scoring fairer or more biased? Research on the role of text features in fair credit scoring, using the LendingClub dataset.

  • Python
  • XGBoost
  • NLP
  • Fairness Metrics

The Research Question

Machine learning has transformed credit underwriting — but greater accuracy can come at the cost of wider gaps between borrower groups. At the same time, research has shown that the free text borrowers write in loan applications carries real predictive signal for default.

This thesis sits at the intersection of the two: it examines whether adding text-derived features to credit-scoring models improves predictive performance, and how it affects fairness across borrower groups — using loan-level data from LendingClub.

Approach

Data

The LendingClub peer-to-peer lending dataset — structured borrower and loan attributes alongside free-text loan descriptions, processed with NLP techniques into model features.

Models

Three model families compared head-to-head: XGBoost, Random Forest and Logistic Regression — trained with and without text features to isolate their contribution.

Fairness Evaluation

Performance is judged not only on accuracy, but under fairness metrics such as Equalized Odds and the Brier Gap, quantifying how error rates and calibration differ between borrower groups.

Why It Matters

Credit-scoring models decide who gets access to credit and at what price. Regulators worldwide — from the EU AI Act to fair-lending supervision — increasingly demand that automated underwriting be not only accurate but explainable and non-discriminatory. Understanding whether alternative data such as text narrows or widens group disparities is a question with direct policy and industry implications.

This research is ongoing as part of my Master's degree; results will be shared here when the work is complete.
